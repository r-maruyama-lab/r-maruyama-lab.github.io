Research Vision

Deciphering Intrapatient Heterogeneity and Evolution in Breast Cancer

Understanding Cancer, One Patient at a Time.

Breast cancer heterogeneity exists not only between patients but also within an individual patient, across tumor regions, anatomical sites, and time. Our research seeks to understand this diversity through detailed investigations of individual clinical cases, integrating single-cell transcriptomics, spatial omics, and genomic information.

Research Philosophy

Depth within patients

We prioritize in-depth investigation of individual patients, examining the molecular, cellular, spatial, and temporal diversity of their tumors. By integrating multiple samples, regions, and molecular modalities, we aim to uncover biological features that may be overlooked by conventional cohort-level analyses.

Validation across patients

Insights derived from detailed individual cases can provide starting points for broader biological investigations. We seek to evaluate these observations in independent clinical samples and publicly available datasets, distinguishing patient-specific phenomena from potentially generalizable biological principles.

Research Themes

  1. Intratumoral Heterogeneity
  2. Spatiotemporal Cancer Evolution
  3. Spatial Organization of Tumors
  4. Computational Methods for Single-Cell and Spatial Omics

Research Theme 01

Intratumoral Heterogeneity

Diversity Within a Single Tumor

Individual breast tumors contain cancer cell populations with diverse genomic and transcriptional characteristics. Such heterogeneity may involve differences in copy-number alterations, gene expression programs, cellular states, and clinically relevant markers such as estrogen receptor (ER) and HER2.

Using single-cell RNA sequencing and spatial transcriptomics, we investigate how distinct cancer cell populations coexist within individual tumors and how their molecular properties relate to one another.

Our goal is to characterize intratumoral diversity beyond conventional tumor classifications, focusing on the biological complexity of each individual tumor.

Research Theme 02

Spatiotemporal Cancer Evolution

How Cancer Changes Across Sites and Time

Cancer evolves under the influence of disease progression, environmental conditions, and therapeutic selection pressures. Comparing multiple lesions and time points from the same patient offers an opportunity to investigate how cancer cell populations change over time and across anatomical sites.

We analyze paired primary and recurrent tumors, as well as longitudinal and metastatic specimens, using integrated genomic, single-cell, and spatial transcriptomic approaches.

Particular attention is given to the possibility that pre-existing minor subclones contribute to treatment resistance and recurrence, and to the distinction between genomic clonal evolution and transcriptional state changes.

Our objective is to understand how intrapatient heterogeneity evolves during cancer progression and treatment.

Research Theme 03

Spatial Organization of Tumors

Understanding Tumor Architecture

The biological properties of a tumor depend not only on the identities and molecular states of its cells, but also on how these cells are spatially organized within tissue.

Using high-resolution spatial transcriptomic technologies, including Xenium and Visium, we investigate the spatial distribution of cancer cell populations, tumor–stroma interfaces, layered tumor architectures, and cellular states associated with necrotic regions.

Beyond discrete cell clustering, we aim to characterize spatially continuous transcriptional changes and localized transitions between cancer cell states.

Through these analyses, we seek to understand how cancer heterogeneity is organized within tissue and how spatial architecture relates to tumor biology.

Research Theme 04

Computational Methods for Single-Cell and Spatial Omics

Developing Tools to Explore Cancer Heterogeneity

Single-cell and spatial omics technologies have made it possible to investigate cellular diversity at unprecedented resolution. However, interpreting these complex datasets remains challenging, particularly when using targeted gene panels or analyzing highly heterogeneous tumor tissues.

To address practical analytical challenges arising from clinical cancer research, we develop computational methods for cell-type annotation, spatial tissue analysis, and the investigation of genomic heterogeneity.

Our ongoing methodological work includes HiSCAT and PanelScope for marker-based cell annotation, SpArchii for spatial tissue architecture analysis, and CNVTracer for investigating copy-number-based relationships among cancer cell populations.

These tools are intended to improve the interpretability of single-cell and spatial omics data and facilitate the discovery of biologically meaningful patterns.

Future Directions

From Individual Tumors to Generalizable Biological Principles

Our future research aims to integrate genomic clonal structure, transcriptional cell states, spatial tissue architecture, and longitudinal changes within individual patients.

By connecting these complementary dimensions, we seek to investigate how cancer heterogeneity arises, persists, and contributes to disease progression and therapeutic resistance.

We also aim to extend findings from deeply characterized individual cases to independent patient cohorts, identifying biological principles that may be shared across tumors while preserving the complexity of individual disease trajectories.

Depth within patients. Validation across patients.
